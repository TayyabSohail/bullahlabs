# Responsible AI Adoption Rulebook

## One-sentence rule

**Only improve work that a team already wants improved, can measure safely, and
can choose to keep or reject after a small pilot.**

This rulebook works in any market. Local privacy, labour and data-protection
rules still apply; the client must use the appropriate local review process.

---

## 1. The operating model

| We are here to do | We are not here to do |
| --- | --- |
| Remove repetitive effort from a real workflow | Make people prove they are using AI |
| Improve speed, quality or consistency | Read prompts, chats or private work |
| Give teams safe, useful ways to use AI | Monitor individual activity or rank employees |
| Test a change before scaling it | Force a habit change because an AI licence exists |
| Keep human judgement where it matters | Automate high-consequence decisions without safeguards |

**Core method: audit the workflow, not the worker.**

## 2. Primary B2B target category

### Start here: freight forwarding and logistics-service firms

| Field | Exact target |
| --- | --- |
| Industry | B2B freight forwarders, customs brokers and third-party logistics providers |
| Company size | 50–300 employees; large enough to have repeatable operations, small enough for a fast decision |
| Department | Shipment operations and customer service |
| End users | Shipment coordinators, customer-service agents, documentation staff and operations supervisors |
| Economic buyer | COO, Head of Operations or Managing Director |
| Internal champion | Operations manager, customer-service manager or continuous-improvement lead |
| Data / tool approver | IT, security or data-protection lead |
| First offer | A four-week Shipment Operations Workflow Audit |

### Why this is the first category

| Requirement | Why logistics operations fit |
| --- | --- |
| People need help now | Teams work under delivery deadlines, manage exceptions and answer repeated status questions |
| The work repeats | Status updates, shipment hand-offs, document checks, customer emails and incident summaries recur every day |
| Numbers already exist | Transport-management, ticketing and CRM systems hold response time, backlog, reopened cases, error and hand-off data |
| Improvement is measurable | Compare time to resolve, first-response time, reopen rate, document-error rate and hand-offs |
| AI can help safely | Assist with summaries, draft updates, SOP retrieval and structured hand-offs while a person approves the result |
| The change is optional | A team can test one workflow card without changing routing, pricing, customs decisions or employment decisions |

### First workflow to sell

| Workflow | Current pain | Safe AI-assisted step | Metrics |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Shipment exception update | Agents manually collect status from several systems and draft repetitive client updates | Create a structured draft from approved, non-sensitive shipment facts; agent checks and sends it | First-response time, time to resolution, reopened cases, customer corrections |

### Explicit exclusions for the first pilot

| Do not automate | Why |
| --- | --- |
| Customs, legal or regulatory decisions | A human specialist must make and verify these decisions |
| Price quotations or contract commitments | Incorrect output can create financial exposure |
| Route or carrier selection | Keep operational judgement and accountability with the team |
| Any use of unapproved customer data | The pilot must use the client's approved tools and data process |

## 3. Other B2B categories, ranked

Choose departments with a visible problem, repeatable work and data already in
their normal systems. Do not choose them because a leader wants an "AI
initiative."

| Rank | B2B category | Exact department to target | Good first workflow | Why it ranks here | Main caution |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Freight forwarders and logistics-service firms | Shipment operations and customer service | Shipment exception update | Clear pain, daily repeated work and operational data already exist | Keep away from customs, price and routing decisions |
| 2 | Industrial distributors and B2B wholesalers | Order management and customer service | Order-status update or product-information response | ERP/CRM data makes backlog and correction rates visible | Do not let AI confirm stock, price or delivery dates without verification |
| 3 | Specialist consultancies | Client delivery, research and PMO | Research brief or proposal first draft | High-value drafting and review work | Billable-hour incentives can create resistance; measure quality and rework, not just speed |
| 4 | Commercial property-management firms | Tenant / client operations | Maintenance enquiry triage or property-update draft | High message volume and standard process steps | System and data quality may be inconsistent |
| 5 | Recruitment and staffing firms | Recruitment operations, not hiring decision-makers | Intake summary, job-description preparation or client update | High-volume workflows and established ATS data | Never score, rank or select candidates with the pilot |

### Category qualification scorecard

Score every condition before offering a pilot. A team needs **all six** green
answers. One red answer means do not start a workflow audit yet.

| Question | Green: startable | Red: do not start yet |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Is there a real pain? | Backlog, delay, rework, missed service level or inconsistent quality is already visible | Only a vague wish to "use AI" |
| Does the work repeat? | 15–25 comparable completed tasks can be sampled | Each task is one-off or incomparable |
| Is baseline data available? | CRM, ticket, project, document-review or quality data exists | Measuring would require spying on people |
| Does the team want help? | Sponsor and working team agree to a small experiment | A leader is imposing it on an unwilling team |
| Can a person check the result? | A human can verify, edit or reject the output | The output becomes an unreviewed decision about a person or customer |
| Can it be done safely? | Approved tools and safe inputs are available | Sensitive data must go into an unapproved tool |

## 4. What evidence can we use?

| Use at workflow / team level | Never collect for this service |
| --- | --- |
| Task type, date, status and owner role | Prompt or chat content |
| Time from task assignment to usable draft or completion | Keystrokes, screen recordings or webcam footage |
| Review rounds, returns, hand-offs and reopenings | Individual token counts or AI-use scores |
| Existing quality checks, error categories and corrections | Productivity rankings or performance-management evidence |
| Volume, backlog and service-level data | Sensitive client or employee data outside an approved process |
| Voluntary, anonymous feedback on the new workflow | Secret tracking or passive browser surveillance |

### Report only this

| Good finding | Bad finding |
| --- | --- |
| "The team's proposal first-draft time fell 18%, while review rounds stayed flat." | "Sara used AI more often than Ahmed." |
| "The workflow was rejected because client-data handling was unclear." | "Employees resisted adoption." |
| "The pilot did not improve quality, so we stopped it." | "We must increase AI compliance." |

## 5. The clean audit: five steps

| Step | What happens | What the team gives us | What the team gets |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Pick one problem | Choose one to three repeated workflows with existing pain | A sponsor and access to workflow-level data | A narrow, safe pilot scope |
| 2. Set the guardrails | Agree what data is allowed, who sees it and when to stop | Approval of the pilot contract | A written no-surveillance commitment |
| 3. Measure the baseline | Sample completed tasks from normal systems before changing anything | Existing CRM, ticket, project or review data | A factual picture of the current workflow |
| 4. Co-design one change | Workers identify the frustrating step and test a workflow card | 45–60 minutes from a small voluntary group | A method built around their real work |
| 5. Pilot, compare, decide | Run a two-week reversible test and compare against baseline | Voluntary use and short feedback | A clear scale, revise or stop decision |

### The pilot contract: required before step 3

| Must be written down | Example |
| --- | --- |
| Workflow in scope | "Prepare a first draft of a client research brief" |
| Decision to be made | "Should this workflow card be offered to the team?" |
| Allowed data sources | CRM timestamps, document review history, QA corrections |
| Explicitly excluded data | Prompts, private chats, screens and individual usage data |
| Audience for results | Sponsor and pilot team receive team-level findings |
| Stop condition | Any safety concern, quality decline or participant withdrawal pattern |
| Data retention | Delete raw pilot extracts after the agreed report window |

## 6. Measure outcomes, not AI activity

Pick at most three measures for a workflow before the pilot starts.

| Workflow | Strong measures | Weak measures to avoid |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Research brief | Time to usable draft; factual corrections; review rounds | Prompts written; tokens consumed |
| Client follow-up | Turnaround time; approval rate; reopened requests | Messages generated |
| Candidate brief | Time to complete; recruiter edits; client returns | Candidate ranking speed |
| Property enquiry | First response time; resolution rate; reopen rate | Automated replies sent |
| Campaign asset | Revision rounds; approval time; brand/compliance errors | Pieces of content published |

### Decision rule after the pilot

| Result | Decision |
| --- | --- |
| Main measure improves by the pre-agreed threshold, quality does not decline, and most participants choose to continue | Scale carefully with the team |
| Improvement is real but quality/safety is uncertain | Revise the workflow card and run another small test |
| No material improvement or the team does not want it | Stop. Keep the baseline learning and do not force rollout |
| Safety, privacy or client-risk issue appears | Stop immediately and escalate through the client's local process |

Default quantitative threshold: **at least 15% improvement in the main
workflow measure**, unless the client has a stronger existing benchmark.

## 7. The workflow card

Every approved method must fit on one page.

| Field | Required answer |
| --- | --- |
| Task | What is being completed? |
| Good use | Which repetitive step may AI assist? |
| Do not use | When must a person do the work without AI? |
| Safe inputs | What may be entered into the approved tool? |
| Forbidden inputs | What data must never be entered? |
| Output standard | What must a usable result contain? |
| Human check | Who verifies accuracy, judgement and client suitability? |
| Escalation | What should someone do when unsure? |

## 8. Language rules

| Say this | Do not say this |
| --- | --- |
| "Which repetitive step would you like to make easier?" | "You need to change how you work." |
| "We will keep this only if it improves the work." | "Everyone must adopt AI." |
| "We will report team workflow outcomes, not individual usage." | "We will monitor adoption." |
| "A person remains responsible for the final result." | "The AI completed it." |
| "Use the smallest suitable tool for the job." | "Save tokens." |
